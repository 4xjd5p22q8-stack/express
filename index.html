Before You Hit the Water - Everything You Need to Know

Read this carefully. By the end, you'll know exactly what to expect, what to wear, and why - so you can focus on having the best time possible.

🚣 What Is White Water Rafting? Tap to expand

White water rafting is a guided group adventure where you paddle an inflatable raft through a river with natural rapids - sections where the water moves fast, drops in elevation, and creates waves and splashes. The Koprulu Canyon river we use is graded Class III, meaning it's thrilling and dynamic, with real waves and turns - but it's perfectly safe for first-timers when you follow your guide's commands. You'll be seated on the sides of the raft with a paddle in hand, working as a team with your crew. Your captain - an experienced guide seated at the rear - controls direction and calls out paddle commands like "Forward!", "Back!", or "Stop!" Your job is simply to listen, paddle, and enjoy the ride.

🗺️ Your 11km Journey Tap to expand

The river section you'll be rafting is 11 kilometers long from start to finish. The first 3 kilometers are the most exciting - this is where the strongest rapids are, and where you'll be paddling hard and working as a team. Around the 3km mark, you'll pull over to a riverside stop for a 15-minute break. Cold drinks and freshly made gozleme - a traditional Turkish flatbread filled with cheese or potato - are available to buy here. It's a great moment to catch your breath, take photos, and refuel. After the break, the remaining 8 kilometers are calmer and more scenic, drifting through the stunning Koprulu Canyon with towering cliffs, crystal-clear water, and lush forest on both sides. This stretch is the perfect time to soak it all in. You'll arrive at base camp by raft, where the rest of your day - lunch, the video, and your tab - awaits.

👟 Why Water Shoes? Tap to expand

Water shoes are one of the most important things you can wear on the river - and here's why. At several points during the trip, you may need to step out of the raft onto slippery, uneven riverbed rocks - whether at the break stop, pushing off at the start, or during any unexpected moments on the water. Regular sandals or flip flops can easily come off in the current and will offer zero grip on wet stone. Bare feet are uncomfortable and risky on rocky terrain. Water shoes grip wet surfaces, protect your feet, drain instantly, and stay securely on your feet even in moving water. If you don't have water shoes, secure sports shoes that you don't mind getting wet are the next best option. Do not wear open-toed sandals, flip flops, or go barefoot.

📱 Why a Waterproof Phone Case? Tap to expand

You're about to spend time on moving water - and water gets everywhere. Splashes from rapids, water dripping from paddles, and the general wet environment of a raft mean that any exposed phone will almost certainly get wet at some point. A standard phone in a pocket or loose bag is not safe on the river. A waterproof phone case - the kind that seals shut and floats - protects your device so you can actually use it on the water, capture moments, and not spend the whole trip worried about your phone. We strongly recommend purchasing or bringing one before the trip. If you choose not to, please leave your phone secured in your vehicle or in our lockers at base camp. Damaged phones are not covered and we want you focused on the fun, not your valuables.

☀️ What to Wear Tap to expand

Wear a swimsuit or light athletic wear underneath - something you're comfortable getting completely wet in. Over that, you can wear a light t-shirt or rash guard. Avoid denim, heavy cotton, or anything that becomes uncomfortable when soaked. You will get wet - plan for it, embrace it. A change of dry clothes waits for you at base camp after the run, so pack a bag with a dry outfit, a towel, and any personal items you'd like access to post-river. Leave anything valuable - wallets, jewelry, fragile items - locked in your vehicle before you depart for the start point.

🛡️ Safety on the Water Tap to expand

Your helmet and life jacket are not optional - they are worn at all times on the water, no exceptions. The life jacket is fitted specifically to keep you buoyant if you ever exit the raft unexpectedly. In the unlikely event you fall in, do not panic. Roll onto your back, keep your feet pointed downstream and raised, and let the current carry you. Your guide will reach you with a paddle or throw rope within seconds. Never try to stand up in fast-moving water - the current can trap your feet between rocks. Every guide on the river is trained in swift-water rescue and first aid. You are in experienced hands. Listen to your captain, follow commands immediately, and you will have a safe and incredible experience.

Everything You Need to Know

🏛️ Selge Ancient City Tap to expand

Selge is one of Turkey's most remarkably preserved ancient cities, founded by the Pisidians and later occupied by the Greeks and Romans. It sits at around 900 metres altitude in the Taurus Mountains, overlooking the Köprülü Canyon. The collapsed colosseum — a Roman theatre — is the centrepiece of the site. It once held thousands of spectators and is still largely intact structurally despite centuries of exposure. Walking through it gives a genuine sense of scale and history that photographs barely capture.

🗿 Adam Kayalar — Avatar Land Tap to expand

Adam Kayalar translates directly as "Man-Rocks" — ancient human-shaped figures carved into the canyon walls, located within Köprülü Canyon National Park along the historic St. Paul walking trail. The carvings are believed to date back to the Pisidian and Roman periods, possibly representing deities or ancestors. Their tall, standing, humanoid shapes rising from the canyon floor have earned them the nickname "Avatar Land" — a reference to the surreal, sci-fi quality of the landscape. Whether you're into history, photography, or just extraordinary views, this stop delivers all three.

🚌 Language Groups & Cabrio Bus Tap to expand

To make sure everyone gets the most from the commentary, guests are grouped by language on arrival and assigned to a specific driver-guide and cabrio bus. You'll stay with the same driver and the same vehicle for the entire day — morning run to Selge and Adam Kayalar, back for lunch, then afternoon run to Eagle Canyon. This keeps the experience personal and the information clear regardless of which language you speak.
